Two technical objections remain to your proposal:
1. Retained free backing CANNOT become an optional product decision while we claim a backing-memory ceiling. It stays charged until returned or through a verified decommit/recommit scheme. Reusable payload credit is distinct from global backing credit. Unused slots can be reused inside already charged pages without an additional global charge; reassigning pages between domains must not double charge or magically erase resident backing.
2. PathIndex.reserve's page_allocator.rawAlloc is NOT a commit-aware reservation mechanism. I inspected installed Zig 0.16.0 /opt/homebrew/Cellar/zig/0.16.0_1/lib/zig/std/heap/PageAllocator.zig:132: mmap uses READ|WRITE, PRIVATE|ANONYMOUS. It returns writable backing with demand faults. First touch is not a software-accounted commit, and rawAlloc has no separate commit notification. We cannot map every safety maximum this way and charge only touched/live slots without defining accounting at shared-page granularity. For an initial implementation charge mapped writable backing in full. Treat explicit VA reserve/activate/deactivate as a separate platform-aware experiment; distinguish VA, charged writable backing, and RSS. Stable segmented pools are an alternative candidate if explicit reservation is not selected. No promise of fault-free operation merely from prefaulting, because OS residency remains outside our control.

Suggested consensus to ratify:
A. One fixed total owner allowance, backing charged exactly once including retained free memory; protected bounded control reserve. GPU/shared/child metrics distinct, shared backing never double-counted.
B. Recommended hybrid policy: mandatory resources stay protected for admitted entities; flexible remainder serves further admissions and optional data. Numeric floors and supported geometry are product choices. Fixed per-domain partition remains a valid alternative, not a prerequisite implied by a budget. Existing admission guarantees aren't withdrawn to admit another entity.
C. Typed reusable pools + compact hot tables; stable identities/generations. Capacity growth and payload backing work happen at controlled admission/preparation boundaries, not steady key/byte/frame loops. Transactional reservation before spawn/publication, including temporary replacement peaks; refuse cleanly if it cannot fit. No moving live records behind borrowed pointers.
D. Move fields into their suitable canonical table when that suffices; duplicate derived representations only for real incompatible consumers with explicit revisions/lifetime and measured read/update/memory benefit.
E. Placement is a measurable policy per pool. No default hardcoded M3 cache geometry and no mandatory coloring for all objects. Adjacency, prefetch and cache conflict explanations require separate evidence. Select a stride/policy only from cross-target experiments.
F. Current wire/ID/platform safety bounds stay until explicitly revised. Integer masks are an implementation choice we may replace; compile-time product maxima are not immutable architecture. Configurable effective counts remain finite and cannot silently exceed wire limits.
G. Preserve artifacts; collect event/dereference counts + inline inventory; cross layout and placement; test pool churn and first-touch/page costs; validate Linux/x86-64 natively before platform-wide claims. Separate validation of repeated search/label preparation and renderer data clusters remains in the roadmap.
